School of the Air (0.30.0), neighbours' calls (0.31.0), the medevac (D'thorga Framework 0.338.0). Nothing is tested in game.

The owner chose all four on 27 Nov 2026, after advice by radio, the Elmer and other factions' doctors
([radio advice and relays](radio-advice-and-relays.md)). Two are from the owner's October radio design
([radio framework design](radio-framework-design.md), Phase 4): "a radio friend and an Elmer" and "School of the Air". Australia's
School of the Air taught children on remote stations by HF radio from 1951, and shared the Royal Flying Doctor Service's network; the
Flying Doctor's aircraft are the medevac's model. The neighbours' calls make other factions' help run both ways.

Every figure marked [A] is an agent default, authored, for the owner to revise; [O] marks the owner's choices.

## A radio friend

A colonist spends recreation chatting by radio with a named person at a friendly settlement in reach. The friendship grows.

- **Who chats** [A]: a free colonist taking recreation, with a hand-held in touch with a station, or able to walk to a powered station's
  desk. The game's recreation picks it like any other, as a social recreation.
- **The friend** [A]: one for each colonist, a person of a faction that is not hostile and has radio (industrial or more advanced), at a
  settlement the colony's stations reach through the link budget and relays, as other factions' doctors are reached. Made once and kept
  in the world, so the same name answers each time; the colony keeps one friend per colonist.
- **What it gives** [O, "Mood, and a little goodwill"]:
  - **A mood lift after each chat** that grows with the friendship [A]: +2 for a new friend, +4 for a friend, +6 for a close friend,
    for a day.
  - **The friendship** grows by each hour of chat [A]: a friend after about 10 hours, a close friend after about 30.
  - **A little goodwill**: a chat of at least an hour raises the friend's faction's goodwill by 1 [A], at most 3 times a quadrum for
    each faction [A].
  - **If the friendship ends** (the friend dies, or their faction turns hostile), the colonist mourns them [A]: -6 for 6 days, and the
    colonist looks for a new friend next time.
- **The four questions.** It lets a colonist with a radio pass recreation in a way that reaches beyond the colony; it stops nothing; it
  changes how the player sees the radio, from a tool to a link with neighbours, and gives masts and relays a social reason; what outside
  notices is the friend's faction (its goodwill) and the friend, a person who can later turn up with their faction.
- **Risk: low** [A]: the only loss is a colonist's mourning when a friend dies or their faction turns hostile, which the player is told
  of. No setting of its own.
- **Cost** [A]: nothing each tick. The game asks a recreation giver only when a colonist picks recreation; it reads the radio net's last
  pass and a reach search kept an hour for the map (the doctors' search, shared). A chat's job adds the friendship a tick, a sum.

## School of the Air

Children learn by radio (needs Biotech, as children do).

- **Radio school** [O, "Our radios count, plus radio lessons"]: one new learning desire, **radio school**, done at our station's desk or
  anywhere on a hand-held in touch with a station, after the game's own Microelectronics, as its radio talking needs:
  - **With nobody to teach**, it is radio talking by our radios: it fills the child's learning as the game's radio talking at a comms
    console does.
  - **With a teacher in radio touch**, it is a lesson: an adult colonist who is free, able to talk and not incapable of social work,
    with a hand-held or at a desk on the map, or **in a convoy whose radio reaches the child's colony**, teaches one of their best four
    skills, as the game's lessons do. The child gains that skill at the game's lesson rate times 0.75 [A] (by radio, not face to face);
    the teacher gains Social at the game's rate, and both get the game's "gave a lesson" and "was taught" thoughts.
  - A teacher on the map stands at the radio while teaching (as an adviser does); a teacher in a convoy needs no job.
- **The four questions.** It lets a colony teach children without a school desk, and a parent away with a convoy still teach their
  child; it stops nothing; it changes play by making a hand-held a schoolbook; what outside notices is the child's growth and the
  teacher's thoughts and Social.
- **Risk: none** [A]. No setting of its own.
- **Cost** [A]: the game asks a learning giver only when a child picks what to learn; it reads the radio net's last pass. A teacher is
  looked for once at the start of each lesson, over the colony's free colonists and the convoys in touch with it.

## Neighbours ask for yours

Now and then a friendly settlement in radio reach asks for the colony's best doctor or mechanic on the radio for an hour.

- **How often** [O, "now and then"]: on average once a fortnight [A, from the card's wording], across all factions; only a faction that
  is not hostile and has radio, with a settlement the colony's stations reach, asks.
- **Who they ask for** [A]: the colony's best Medicine or Construction (whichever is higher), at 6 or more, free and in radio touch.
- **The letter**: says who asks for whom and why; **yes** sends the colonist to the radio for an hour and earns **5 goodwill** [O, the
  card's figure], and the colonist gains a little of that skill and Social [A: as an adviser's Social, and the skill at half the rate];
  **no** costs nothing [O]. Unanswered, the request lapses after 6 hours [A].
- **A setting**: "Neighbours ask for help by radio", on by default [A]: some players will not want the letters.
- **The four questions.** It lets the player earn goodwill with a skilled colonist's time; it stops the colonist working for an hour;
  it changes play by giving a good doctor or mechanic a reputation beyond the colony; what outside notices is the faction (goodwill)
  and the colonist (skill, Social).
- **Risk: none** [A]: the player chooses each time.
- **Cost** [A]: one chance roll an hour; a reach search only when the roll asks.

## The flying doctor flies: a medevac

A convoy calls home by radio, and an aircraft with stretchers flies out, takes the downed aboard and brings them home.

- **The button** [O, "A convoy button"]: **Call a medevac**, on a convoy whose radio reaches home with someone downed in it. It is
  refused, with the reason, when the convoy is out of touch, nobody is downed, or the colony it reaches has no aircraft ready.
- **Which aircraft** [A]: at the colony the convoy reaches, a player's aircraft standing on the map with stretchers fitted (the litter
  kit or the C-27J's stretchers), its crew aboard, and the fuel to fly there and back with the airdrop's margin; the nearest to the
  convoy if several. **Its crew must already be aboard** [A]: the button never sends colonists to board, so the player keeps control
  of who flies.
- **The flight** (reusing the airdrop's flight to a convoy and its way home): the aircraft flies to the convoy's tile, touches down
  there with no map (as the airdrop turns round in the air), moves the downed colonists and animals of the convoy onto its stretchers,
  as many as it has room for, the worst wounded first [A], and flies home to the spot it left.
- **At home** it sets the patients down beside it at the next tick, and the colony's doctors take them to bed as the game does; the
  litter's "take casualties aboard" is ignored for them for an hour [A], so nobody carries them straight back aboard.
- **The four questions.** It lets the player save a downed colonist far from home without turning the convoy round; it stops nothing
  (the aircraft is away while it flies); it changes play by giving stretchers, fuel and radio reach a reason to be ready together; what
  outside notices is the colony's hospital, which fills from afar, and the aircraft's fuel and flying time.
- **Risk: low** [A]: the aircraft flies as any flight of ours does, with the airdrop's fuel margin; a convoy that moves on before it
  arrives is not met, and the aircraft flies home. No setting of its own: nothing happens unless the player presses the button.
- **Cost** [A]: nothing each tick. The button's checks are kept a tenth of an hour for a convoy; a flight costs what any flight costs.
- **Not built yet**: medevac from a map (a camp or a site), which the litter's own loading already covers by hand.
