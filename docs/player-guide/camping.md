# Camping and life on the road

How to camp beside a vehicle, sleep in or on it, and keep its people fed, watered and in good spirits on a long trip: tents,
berths, camp gear, the kitchen, water, lights and life aboard. It applies to **every Vehicle Framework land vehicle from any mod**
(the deck tent only to those with a flat deck), with the gear made by **D'thorga Framework**. [Back to the guide](README.md).

## Read this first (gotchas)

- **Not yet tested in game.** Everything here is built and checked offline; none of it has been seen in a real colony.
- **A vehicle cannot drive off while its camp is set up, or while anyone is in its deck tent.** Pack up first.
- **Driving through the night is off by default** (D'thorga Framework's settings, vehicle systems). **Put two drivers in every
  vehicle** if you turn it on: one vehicle with nobody to take over stops the whole caravan for the night.
- **Water needs Dubs Bad Hygiene.** Without it, thirst and washing don't exist, and the crew water tank and camp washing are not offered.
- **Tanks and stores never fill themselves**: the crew water tank, a lantern's chemfuel, the kitchen box's fuel. Fill them before you go.
- **Two settings are on by default** and can be switched off: **Walk-in cabins** (the deck tent and the Osprey's cabin) and
  **Hotboxing** (smoking aboard is shared in a closed cab).

## Camp accessories: fridge, house battery and power outlet

Research **vehicle electrics**, make the kit at the **vehicle parts bench**, put it in the vehicle's cargo and fit it from the
fittings window. Every land vehicle has a **fridge bay**, a **house battery tray** and an **outlet panel** for them. Taking one off puts
the kit back in the cargo.

| Accessory | What it does | What it costs to run |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **12-volt fridge** (20 kg) | Keeps up to 30 kg of the cargo's food at 4 °C, choosing what would spoil first. It has its own switch | 0.2 to 1.5 A, more in the heat |
| **House battery** (25 kg) | A second 100 Ah battery for the fridge and outlet, so they never flatten the battery that starts the engine. Below a tenth it cuts them off | Charges while driving once the starting battery is full, or on shore power |
| **Power outlet** (4 kg) | Switched on while parked, the conduit beneath the vehicle powers lamps, a radio or a bench wired to it, up to 300 W (600 W on a 24 V truck) | What it gives, in amps: a 200 W radio is about 20 A on 12 V |

- **Without a house battery, the fridge and outlet drain the starting battery with the engine off.** A fridge alone flattens a stock
  battery in under two days parked; the vehicle warns when the battery gets too weak to start.
- **To run a camp longer, leave the engine idling**: the outlet keeps its conduit down and the alternator carries it, burning fuel.
- **The outlet gives only what its devices ask**, and never fills a colony's batteries. Switched on, the vehicle draws nothing from
  that grid.
- Each accessory takes its weight off the cargo capacity.

## Camping: awning, roof tent and swags

**The vehicle cannot drive off while its camp is set up.** Pack up first. Not yet tested in game.

- **Make the gear.** An **awning** at a tailoring bench and a **roof tent** at the vehicle parts bench (research **vehicle parts**
  for both); fit them from the fittings window. Every land vehicle has a **side rail** for the awning and a **roof mount** for the
  roof tent. A **swag** is built like a bedroll (Architect, D'thorga, Motor pool); uninstall it and load it into the cargo. The
  game's own bedrolls in the cargo count too.
- **Set up camp.** Select the parked vehicle and press **Set up camp**. Hover over the button first: the ground shows where
  everything will go. A colonist does the work (construction). **Camp on the left/right** picks the side, as the driver sees it; if
  that side is blocked, the camp goes on the other.
- **What you get:**

| Piece | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Awning** (15 kg) | A canvas roof over two rows along the camp's side, on poles. Rain and snow stay off; it is no room and keeps no cold out |
| **Roof tent** (55 kg) | A bed for two under its own canvas, at the rear of the camp's side. As restful as a real bed, dry in rain. Waking in it gives **slept in a roof tent** (+4) |
| **Swag** (6 kg) | A better bedroll: rest 1.0, comfort 0.75. Caravans sleep on it. At home it takes the bedroom furniture a bedroll takes. Waking from a fair, mild night in one with open sky overhead gives **slept under the stars** (+5); not under the awning, not in rain or cold |

**More tents** (not yet tested in game). Each is a fitting made from a kit at a tailoring bench, after **vehicle parts** research:

| Tent | Goes on | What stands at camp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Side fly** (6 kg) | Side rail | A tarp over three rows at the rear of the camp's side, on two poles. Cheaper and lighter than the awning, and shorter. No room |
| **Walled awning** (33 kg; an awning plus **awning walls**) | Side rail | A **closed canvas room** along the camp's side, four cells out and as long as the vehicle, with a door flap |
| **Rear tent** (25 kg) | Roof mount or side rail | A **closed canvas room** behind the vehicle, a cell wider than it on each side and five deep. **Unhitch any trailer first** |
| **Fold-out camper**, compact (90 kg) or roomy (110 kg, comfier beds, slower to fit), from one kit | The Pintle flatbed's tent mount | A **closed canvas room** beside the trailer, five cells out and six along, with **two double beds** and a walkway |

- **The side rail takes one of** the awning, the side fly, the walled awning or the rear tent; **the roof mount one of** the roof tent,
  the rear tent or (on the flatbed) the fold-out camper.
- **A closed tent is a real room**: beds inside are indoors, and the wind and rain stay out. **Swags and gear are set out inside it
  first**, leaving the cell inside the flap clear. Want a swag under the stars instead? Move it out once, and it goes back there.
- **Camp heater** (8 kg; Architect, D'thorga, Motor pool; 40 steel and a component): uninstall it, fill it with chemfuel and load it.
  The camp sets it out inside a closed tent, where it keeps the tent near **15 °C**, burning about 2 chemfuel a day and nothing while
  the tent is warmer. Under an open awning or fly it warms nothing.
- **Taking down any one wall brings that tent's canvas roof down**, as an awning's pole does.

**Life aboard** (not yet tested in game): people riding in a vehicle pass the time by themselves. Riders who are not driving drink from the
crew water tank when thirsty, read a **book** from the cargo or their packs, or have a **treat** (a tea, a coffee, something sweet). Pack
books and tea for a long trip.

- **Cab stereo** (vehicle parts bench, research **vehicle electrics**; fitted in the stereo slot): music for everyone aboard while it plays.
  About 1 amp; its switch is on the vehicle.
- **Cab reading light** (fitted in the cab light mount): riders can read after dark. A third of an amp.
- **Smoking aboard** (setting **Hotboxing**, on): a rider whose drug policy allows a smoked drug may light up. With the cab closed and the
  **windows up**, everyone aboard gets a share and remembers it, good or bad; a high driver drives slower. **Windows down** (a switch on the
  vehicle) clears the smoke, but loses the cab's warmth or cool.

**Crew water tank** (with Dubs Bad Hygiene; not yet tested in game): make the kit at the vehicle parts bench (research **vehicle parts**)
and fit it in the vehicle's **water tank bay**. It holds **120 litres**.

- **On the road** the crew drink and wash from it. **Parked with the engine off**, it joins the plumbing beneath the vehicle: **park beside
  plumbing or a water bowser to fill it**. It never fills by itself. A message, and the Fleet board, say when it is down to a quarter.
- **Washing at camp:** uninstall a Dubs **basin** and **simple shower** into the cargo with the camp gear. The camp sets them out, and the pipes
  under the camp join them to the tank. A shower takes **65 litres** and is cold (no hot water tank at camp).

**Camp lights** (not yet tested in game): build them in the Architect (D'thorga, Motor pool; research **vehicle parts**) and pack them
in the cargo with the camp gear. Both light themselves at dusk.

- **Camp lantern** (2 kg): burns about **one chemfuel a night**, only while lit, and warms the air a little. Needs no vehicle. Fill it before you go.
- **12-volt camp light** (4 kg): runs from the vehicle's battery, about 10 watts each. The vehicle gets a **Camp lights** switch; with it off,
  or the battery flat, they go dark. Set down away from a camp, it has no power.

**Kitchen box, table and stools** (not yet tested in game): build them in the Architect (D'thorga, Motor pool; research
**vehicle parts**), uninstall them and load them into the cargo with the rest of the camp gear.

- **Kitchen box** (18 kg): a camp stove. Set out with the camp, it takes the same bills as a stove at home, and with Vanilla Brewing
  Expanded's Coffees and Teas it also brews **tea and coffee**. It burns **chemfuel or wood**, only while cooking; fill it before you go.
- **Camp table** (5 kg) and **camp stools** (2 kg each): the stools are set down **beside the table, facing it**, up to four, so people
  sit down to eat.
- **On the road:** a kitchen box packed in a caravan's cargo cooks at night like the Billy, **up to 8 meals a cook** (the Billy makes
  20, and is used first if you have both).

**The deck tent** (not yet tested in game; needs the **Walk-in cabins** setting, which is on by default): for a vehicle with a **flat
deck**: our **Talus tipper** and **Pintle flatbed**, and, where their mods are active, Vanilla Vehicles Expanded's **Mule** and
**Roadrunner**, Frontline Workshop's **Land Cruiser J70** and RimThunder's **M1075 HEMTT**. Fit it to the roof mount. It stands **on the deck**, so it
needs no ground and no camp set up. **One kit, two ways to fit it:** **light** (30 kg) or **roomy** (40 kg, slower to fit, 2 degrees
warmer inside and comfier beds).

- **Go in:** right-click the vehicle with a colonist. Inside is a small room with **two beds** and a flap that leads back out
  (one bed on the Land Cruiser, four on the HEMTT).
- **Sleepers:** press **Deck tent: nobody** on the vehicle and choose as many colonists as it has beds. **At bedtime they walk in by themselves**,
  and walk out when rested.
- **Warmth:** canvas only. The air inside is that around the vehicle, about 4 °C warmer in the cold, never warmed past 15 °C.
- **Danger wakes them:** a raid, or the vehicle on fire, wakes everyone inside and sends them out, with a message.
- **The vehicle will not move while anyone is inside.** The cab's seats are not affected by people in the tent. If you form a
  caravan with someone inside, they come out and ride aboard as it leaves.

- **On our own vehicles the gear shows:** a closed roof tent on the cab (or the flatbed's deck), the rolled bag of an awning, side fly,
  walled awning or rear tent on the side rail (on the side the camp is pitched; a rear tent on the roof lies across it), the deck tents
  on the tipper's and flatbed's decks, and the fold-out camper on the flatbed. Other mods' vehicles are never drawn on.
- **Moved a swag by hand?** It goes back to that spot next time. **Forget the camp's layout** starts afresh.
- **Pack up camp** rolls everything back into the vehicle. It waits for sleepers to get up. Anything you moved away from the camp,
  and anything lying under the awning, stays where it is.
- **Leaving with the camp out** (forming a caravan): it is packed as the vehicle leaves; a bed still in use stays behind.
- **If the vehicle is destroyed**, its camp stays as ordinary beds and poles. Taking a pole down brings the awning's canvas down.
- **Other factions' vehicles** at their own bases may be found with their camp out. Capture one and its camp is yours to pack.
- **The camper trailer** (not yet tested in game): the **Pintle flatbed** takes camp gear too. Fit a roof tent and an awning from its
  fittings window (it has a **tent mount** and a **side rail**), load swags into its cargo, tow it out and **unhitch it**: its own
  **Set up camp** then pitches the camp beside the trailer, and the truck is free to drive off. **Pack up before hitching it again.**
  **Tank trailers, the Billy and the shower trailer camp too**, from a **side rail** only: an awning and swags beside them, nothing
  on top.

## Sleeping on the road: cab bunks and driving through the night

Not yet tested in game.

- **Cab bunk** (40 kg; steel and cloth; research **vehicle parts**): fit it to a heavy truck's **sleeper berth** (wheeled, 3,500 kg
  or more). While the caravan travels, the most tired passenger sleeps in it and arrives rested; stopped, it is one more bed. Drivers
  never take it. In a cab of three or more seats it takes one seat's place.
- **Drive through the night** (a setting, **off by default**; D'thorga Framework's settings, under vehicle systems): vehicle caravans
  keep going at night while every vehicle has a rested driver. When a driver tires, a rested passenger of the same vehicle who can
  drive takes the wheel, and a message says who. **Put two drivers in every vehicle**: if any one vehicle has nobody to take over,
  the whole caravan stops for the night as usual.
- Each caravan has its own **Drive through the night** button, on once the setting is on; switch it off to rest that caravan at night.
- **What it costs:** fuel for the extra hours, headlights at night, and tired people at the far end if bunks are short.
- **The time to arrive** counts the road only while the caravan can drive on (its switch on, and someone to take over in every
  vehicle). Planning a route and forming a caravan show it the same way while the setting is on.
- **Sleeping in a parked truck:** on a truck with a cab bunk, press **Bunk: nobody** and choose a colonist. At bedtime they climb
  into the cab and sleep there while it stands still, and climb out rested or when their workday starts
  (**slept in the cab**, +2). Danger, fire or illness wakes them at once. Sick colonists keep their real beds.
  **For a warm cab on a cold night, turn on the Night heater at camp setting and the truck's night heater switch**: it then burns a
  little fuel (0.4 chemfuel an hour) while someone sleeps aboard. Without it, a parked cab is as cold as the night within about an
  hour and a half.

## Food and water on the road

Not yet tested in game.

- **Food in a towed trailer is eaten** once the caravan's own packs run out, and it **counts in the caravan's days of food**, on the
  world map and in the window for forming a caravan.
- **Water in any tank is drunk and washed with** (with Dubs Bad Hygiene): a water bowser, the shower trailer, the Billy's tank, a crew
  water tank. A drink is 1.5 litres; a wash 5; a shower 20 when the shower trailer is along, **hot only while its water heater has
  chemfuel** (a quarter of a unit a shower).
- **Cooking on the road:** the **Billy** field kitchen or a packed **kitchen box** cooks at night from the raw food the caravan carries,
  trailers included. Driving through the night, the kettle simmers on the move; switch on **Meal halts** on the caravan for an hour's
  stop instead. Details are in [the Billy's section](working-vehicles.md#billy-field-kitchen).

## Walk-in cabins

The **Osprey's cabin** is a room you walk into (setting **Walk-in cabins**, on): right-click the aircraft with colonists selected. It
is as warm or cold as its cab, so its heater and air conditioning matter, and **anyone hungry inside eats from the aircraft's cargo**,
in flight too. Details are in [the Osprey's section](working-vehicles.md#v-22-osprey). The deck tent, above, is the same kind of room.

## The logbook and the Fleet board

- **The vehicle's logbook** (its Log tab, or the Fleet board's pane) notes each camp made, each night driven through and who was at the
  wheel, changes of driver, meals cooked on the road, tanks running dry, cold showers, and sleepers woken by danger.
- **The Fleet board** (the fleet button on the bottom bar; give it a key in Options, Keyboard, main tabs, "toggle fleet tab") says
  when a convoy is **camped for the night**, **halted for a meal** or **driving through the night**, when a vehicle's camp is pitched,
  and when a convoy has **less than a day of food** or a crew water tank is low.
